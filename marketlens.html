03 · Three Underlying-Price Scenarios

Assume the stock falls 3%, stays unchanged, or rises 3%, while also applying the IV and time assumptions. Approx. premium = entry + Delta×ΔS + ½Gamma×ΔS² + Vega×ΔIV + Theta×days.

These approximations are most useful for small moves when Greeks remain reasonably stable. Large moves, near-expiration behavior, and large IV changes can create major estimation error. Actual execution also depends on liquidity.

05 · Why This Analysis Can Be Wrong

The direction can be right while the option still loses money

Falling IV, time decay, or a wide bid–ask spread can offset gains from the underlying move.

A stock breakout does not equal trend confirmation

Intraday false breakouts, macro news, earnings expectations, and weak volume can all change the price path.

A stop price does not guarantee the execution price

Gaps, delayed quotes, low liquidity, and wide spreads can cause the actual exit price to differ from the intended level.
